Is Now a Good Time to Buy a House in the Antelope Valley?

A clear summary of what this guide covers and who it is for.

By Nick Armijo August 14, 2026

Is Now a Good Time to Buy in Palmdale?

If you're wondering whether 2026 is a good time to buy a house in the Antelope Valley, the answer depends more on your budget, financing, and long-term plans than trying to perfectly time the housing market. Current data shows home values in Palmdale and Lancaster have remained relatively stable, which may give prepared buyers an opportunity to explore the market without the rapid price growth seen in previous years.

What Is the Antelope Valley Housing Market Like in 2026?

The Antelope Valley housing market in 2026 is relatively stable, with home values in both Palmdale and Lancaster slightly lower than they were a year ago. As of June 2026, Zillow reports a typical home value of approximately $505,741 in Palmdale, down 0.2% from the previous year. Lancaster's typical home value is approximately $467,567, down 0.9% year over year. Homes in both cities were going pending in approximately 27 days, meaning desirable homes can still move quickly even though overall prices have remained relatively steady. For buyers, that makes preparation important. Knowing your budget, financing options, preferred neighborhoods, and must-have features before beginning your search can put you in a stronger position when the right property becomes available.

Are Home Prices Going Down in Palmdale and Lancaster?

Home values in Palmdale and Lancaster have decreased slightly year over year, but current data does not show a dramatic decline in either market. Zillow reported Palmdale's typical home value at about $505,741 in June 2026 compared with approximately $467,567 in Lancaster. The difference also demonstrates why buyers shouldn't treat the entire Antelope Valley as one housing market. Your budget may produce very different options depending on whether you're searching in Palmdale, Lancaster, Quartz Hill, or another Antelope Valley community. Instead of waiting solely for prices to fall, buyers should consider the complete cost of purchasing a home, including the purchase price, mortgage rate, down payment, taxes, insurance, closing costs, and expected length of ownership.

Should I Wait for Home Prices to Drop Before Buying?

Waiting for home prices to drop does not guarantee that buying a home will become more affordable. Home affordability depends on several variables at the same time. A lower purchase price can be offset by different mortgage rates, while increased buyer competition can affect negotiating power and the number of homes available. A better question is: Does buying a home make financial sense for me right now? If you have stable finances, understand your monthly payment, plan to remain in the home long enough for ownership to fit your goals, and find a property within your budget, purchasing may make sense even without perfectly timing the bottom of the market. If the numbers don't work yet, waiting and preparing may be the smarter decision.

Is Now a Good Time to Buy a House in Palmdale?

For some buyers, 2026 may be a good time to buy a house in Palmdale, particularly if they are financially prepared and find a property that fits their long-term needs. Palmdale home values were nearly flat year over year as of June 2026, while available inventory gives buyers multiple properties to consider. However, individual homes and neighborhoods can behave differently from citywide averages. That's why I recommend looking at your specific situation rather than trying to make a decision based on headlines about the national housing market. The Antelope Valley is its own local market. What is happening nationally—or even elsewhere in Los Angeles County—doesn't necessarily tell you what is happening with the homes you're considering in Palmdale or Lancaster.
